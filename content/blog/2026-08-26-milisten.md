Yesterday I vibecoded [milisten](https://github.com/gwicho38/milisten). It turns articles, papers and PDFs into chaptered audiobooks — with the voice running locally, on the machine in front of me.

That's the post. The rest is why.

## What I Actually Wanted

Something light. You hand it a long article, a paper, a PDF, and you listen while you work (or drive, or do the dishes). That is the whole feature. Not a library, not a subscription, not a social layer, not a queue that syncs to a phone nobody wants to look at. The thing you were going to read should just become sound.

## Surely This Exists?

It does. Probably many times over, and better than this.

But the ones I actually tried all had the same shape — the text goes out to somebody's API, audio comes back, and you are billed per character. That is fine for a paragraph. It is less fine when the thing you want to listen to is a ninety-page rule, and less fine again when there are four of those in a week.

So that became the requirement I would not drop: the speech has to happen here. No key, no meter, no round trip.

## The First Attempt

A page anyone can open — [it is here](https://gwicho38.github.io/milisten/). No server, no account, no key.

Paste a link or drop a PDF, then press play. The voice is Kokoro-82M (about 86 MB, downloaded once, then cached and run in a worker on your own machine), and after that first load it keeps working with the network switched off. The first load is slow. I've made peace with it.

## The Part I Did Not Expect

> Text-to-speech is solved. Reading *real documents* aloud is not.

Hand a reader app `91 Fed. Reg. 24968 (7 May 2026)` and you get "ninety-one fed reg twenty-four thousand nine hundred sixty-eight." URLs come out spelled letter by letter. Wherever a PDF wrapped a line, two sentences run together and you lose the thread.

So most of milisten turns out not to be the voice at all — it is a normalization pass sitting between pulling the text out and speaking it, expanding citations, breaking up acronyms, and repairing the line endings a PDF left behind. How aggressive that pass gets is a setting (a good voice needs much less help than a robotic one).

## Where It Is

Vibecoded means vibecoded — one day, a lot of churn, tests clustered exactly where I got burned. There is a CLI that writes m4b files with chapters, a local web UI, and the browser page above. Whether any of it survives contact with next week is genuinely open.

But I listened to two papers with it this morning, so.
